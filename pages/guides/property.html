Property investor guide

Buying property in Ghana as a foreigner

Non-citizens can lease land in Ghana but cannot own it outright. Here is what that means, the steps in order, and what to prepare before you pay anyone.

Facts as of 18.11.2026

General information, not legal, tax or immigration advice. Confirm with the authority or a licensed Ghanaian adviser.

01

The rule that shapes everything

Non-citizens cannot hold freehold: leasehold only, for a maximum of 50 years at any one time. An attempted freehold grant to a non-citizen is void. Source: Land Act 2020 (Act 1036), section 10. As of 18.11.2026.

  • A company with more than 40% foreign ownership is treated as non-Ghanaian for land purposes. Source: Land Act 2020 (Act 1036).
  • Most land is customary (stool, skin, clan or family land). No new freehold can be created over it.
  • Renewal: non-Ghanaians are reported to get no automatic renewal unless the lease says so. We have not verified this against the statute, so check it with a Ghanaian lawyer.
  • Whether foreigners can own an apartment unit: we have not confirmed the legal framework, so ask a Ghanaian lawyer before you rely on it.

02

Step by step

  1. Get a licensed Ghanaian lawyer

    Do this before you pay a deposit or sign anything. Land and company law need local legal counsel; our fee does not cover lawyer fees and we do not act as your lawyer.

  2. Verify the title

    Check who really holds the land before any money moves. Title on customary land can involve several families or a stool; your lawyer searches the records at the Lands Commission.

  3. Agree a lease of up to 50 years

    Negotiate the term, ground rent and any renewal right in writing. A longer term than 50 years at one time is not available to you.

  4. Register the lease

    Register the lease at the Lands Commission. Registration is what protects you if the land is later sold or disputed.

  5. Decide: buy personally or through a company

    A company needs at least 2 directors, one ordinarily resident in Ghana, and a qualified company secretary. Remember the 40% foreign-ownership rule above.

  6. Pay in the right currency

    Residents may not price or take payment for property in foreign currency. Foreign-currency invoices are only for expatriates or non-residents, paid into a Foreign Exchange Account. Source: Bank of Ghana notices 2025/26.

03

What to prepare

  • Valid passport (6 months or more beyond departure) and, if you visit, an e-visa or other entry permission (see below).
  • A shortlist of properties and the seller's identity and title documents, for your lawyer to check.
  • Funds route: a Foreign Exchange Account if you pay as a non-resident.
  • If using a company: names of at least 2 directors (one resident in Ghana), a company secretary, and beneficial-owner details at the 20% threshold.
  • Security awareness: burglaries are reported in expatriate areas (Airport Residential, Cantonments, Ridge, Kokrobite). Ask about security when you view.

05

If you invest through a company

  • The Ghana Investment Promotion Centre Act 2026 (Act 1173, 15.07.2026) removed the minimum capital for most foreign investors. Trading enterprises need US$500,000 in cash equity and 75% skilled Ghanaian staff.
  • Register with GIPA after incorporation; the registration is renewed annually.
  • Beneficial ownership is disclosed at a 20% threshold.

06

Visiting to view properties

  • E-visa online since 25.05.2026 (evisa.immigration.gov.gh). Standard single entry US$260 for non-African nationals; African nationals pay no fee. Processing is quoted at 48 to 96 hours depending on the source, so allow a week or more. Enter within 90 days of issue; stay up to 60 days, set at the border. Approval does not guarantee entry.
  • Visa on arrival was replaced by the May 2026 reform. Do not rely on it: you risk being denied boarding.
  • Yellow fever certificate for all arrivals aged 9 months and over: valid for life, vaccinate at least 10 days before travel. Source: CDC; TravelHealthPro (28.10.2026).
  • Online Health Declaration, mandatory since 12.11.2026, completed before departure: ehds.ghs.gov.gh/declaration.
  • Malaria risk across Ghana: ask a travel clinic about prophylaxis.
  • Passport valid for 6 months or more (apply the stricter rule: beyond your departure date).
  • Cash over US$10,000 must be declared on form FX-5 (since 01.09.2025).
  • Emergency numbers: 112; police 191; ambulance 193; fire 192.
