Doing business in Ghana: enter, work, register

A business visa covers meetings, consultations and advice. Hands-on work needs a work permit and a residence permit. This guide walks the route, with every fact dated and sourced.

Step by step

  1. Check your passport and visa route

    Your passport should be valid for 6 or more months, counted beyond your departure date. Ordinary passports of Canada, the UK, all EU states, China, India, Japan, South Korea, the Philippines, Vietnam, Malaysia, Pakistan and Bangladesh need a visa. ECOWAS and AES states are visa-free. Visa on arrival was replaced by the May 2026 reform, so do not rely on it: you risk being denied boarding.

  2. Apply for the e-visa online

    The e-visa has been online since 25.05.2026 at evisa.immigration.gov.gh. The standard single-entry fee is US$260. Higher tiers are not confirmed: check the portal. You cannot edit an application after submitting, and you must answer information requests within 72 hours. Business visitors usually get up to 60 days.

  3. Complete the Health Declaration

    Mandatory since 12.11.2026, online before departure. Bring your yellow fever certificate: required for all arrivals aged 9 months or more, valid for life, and the vaccine should be given 10 or more days before travel.

  4. Plan equipment and customs

    Laptops and phones are personal effects. Servers and demo kit go through the Red channel on temporary admission with a guarantee, and must leave unchanged within 90 days. An ATA Carnet is not accepted. Use a licensed customs agent. Declare cash over US$10,000 on form FX-5.

  5. Understand the tax position

    Pay for work done in Ghana is taxable from day one, with no minimum number of days. The non-resident rate is a flat 25%. Residence means 183 or more days in any 12 months. Treaties are in force with Belgium, the Czech Republic, Denmark, France, Germany, Italy, Mauritius, Morocco, the Netherlands, Qatar, Singapore, South Africa, Switzerland and the UK; none with Canada, China, India, Japan, Korea, the Philippines, Vietnam, Malaysia, Pakistan or Bangladesh. Speak to a tax adviser.

  6. If you will work or stay: permits

    Hands-on work needs a work permit and a residence permit under the Immigration Act 2000 (Act 573). The company applies. Allow at least 15 to 20 working days. Processing time and validity of the permits are not confirmed: check with the Ghana Immigration Service.

  7. If you will set up a company

    Registration basics are below. Registration with GIPA follows incorporation and is renewed annually. Take advice from a licensed Ghanaian lawyer before you commit.

What to prepare

  • Passport valid 6+ months beyond departure, and a photo under 3 months old
  • Return ticket or itinerary, and proof of accommodation
  • Yellow fever certificate
  • Employer letter, and an invitation letter from your Ghanaian host (our Business plan includes a template)
  • For a work permit: GIPA registration, tax clearance, audited accounts, a Ghana Immigration Clinic medical, a home-country police report, CV and certificates, your contract, a ministry support letter and a Non-Citizen Ghana Card
  • Working hours: private firms usually keep 08:00 to 17:00 Monday to Friday; government offices often close by 15:00 to 16:00

Company registration basics

  • The GIPA Act 2026 (Act 1173, 15.07.2026) removed minimum capital for most foreign investors. Trading enterprises need US$500,000 cash equity and 75% skilled Ghanaian staff.
  • A company needs at least 2 directors, one of them ordinarily resident in Ghana, and a qualified company secretary.
  • Beneficial ownership is declared at a 20% threshold.
  • Stamp duty of 1% applies on a company's stated capital.
  • If you plan to buy land through a company: one with more than 40% foreign ownership is treated as non-Ghanaian for land, so leasehold rules apply.
